TECHNICAL BULLETIN: ENVIRONMENTAL AND ECOLOGICAL BENEFITS OF URBAN FORESTS
Source: Environmental Protection and Urban Planning Agency (2026)

1. Climate Regulation and Cooling
Urban areas suffer from the Urban Heat Island (UHI) effect, where concrete and asphalt absorb and re-emit solar radiation, causing elevated temperatures compared to rural surroundings. Trees mitigate this effect through two main processes: shading and evapotranspiration. Leaf canopies intercept incoming solar radiation, cooling the air and building surfaces underneath. Transpiration absorbs sensible heat from the environment to convert water to vapor, lowering ambient temperatures by several degrees.

2. Air Quality and Particulate Filtration
Trees act as living filters that intercept airborne particulate matter (PM2.5 and PM10) and absorb gaseous pollutants like nitrogen dioxide (NO2), sulfur dioxide (SO2), and ozone (O3) through leaf stomata. Species with high leaf area indexes and rough or hairy leaves are particularly efficient at trapping dust and soot. This biological filtration significantly improves public health in heavily trafficked urban centers.

3. Runoff Mitigation and Hydrology
Tree crowns intercept rainfall, reducing the velocity and volume of water hitting the ground. This delays peak storm runoff, reducing strain on municipal storm drainage systems. Furthermore, tree root networks improve soil infiltration capacity, allowing rainwater to recharge local groundwater tables rather than creating surface flooding.

4. Biodiversity and Wildlife Support
Even single urban trees provide crucial micro-habitats for birds, insects, and small mammals. Tree canopies offer nesting sites and shelter, while flowers, fruits, and seeds serve as food sources. Creating green corridors through urban parks and street trees helps connect fragmented habitats, preserving urban biodiversity.
