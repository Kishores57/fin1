ENCYCLOPEDIA OF TROPICAL URBAN TREES: BIODIVERSITY, MEDICINAL PROPERTIES, AND HABITAT SUPPORT
Publisher: Academic Press of Ecological Sciences (2025)

SECTION 1: AZADIRACHTA INDICA (NEEM)
1.1 Ecological Profile & Habitat Support
Azadirachta indica, commonly known as Neem, is a major keystone species in tropical urban ecosystems. Its dense, spreading canopy provides nesting space, thermal shelter, and roosting sites for over 70 species of birds, as well as bats and insects. The tree's flowers are highly honey-yielding and attract numerous wild bees, butterflies, and other pollinators, supporting urban biodiversity corridors.
1.2 Medicinal & Pharmacological Impact
Neem is renowned for its extensive medicinal properties. The leaves, bark, and seeds contain limonoids (such as azadirachtin, nimbin, and salannin). Historically and in modern pharmacology, Neem extracts exhibit strong antibacterial, antifungal, antiviral, and anti-inflammatory properties. It is widely used in dermatology for wound healing and skin disorders, and its oil acts as a natural, biodegradable insect repellent and organic biopesticide, replacing harmful synthetic chemicals.

SECTION 2: SARACA ASOCA & POLYALTHIA LONGIFOLIA (ASHOKA)
2.1 Ecological Profile & Habitat Support
The Ashoka tree is a critical component of forest and urban greenbelts. Its dense, evergreen foliage acts as an acoustic shield (reducing urban noise pollution) and provides year-round thermal cover for canopy-dwelling birds and arboreal mammals. The flowers are rich in nectar, serving as a primary food source for native honeybees, sunbirds, and butterflies.
2.2 Medicinal & Pharmacological Impact
In traditional Ayurvedic medicine, the bark of Saraca asoca is highly sacred and therapeutic, primarily used for uterine disorders, internal bleeding, and inflammation. The bark contains high concentrations of tannins, flavonoids, and sterols which exhibit uterine-tonic, analgesic, and antimicrobial actions. Furthermore, Polyalthia longifolia (False Ashoka) leaves show significant antibacterial activity and are studied for their cytotoxic potential against pathogens.

SECTION 3: HEVEA BRASILIENSIS (RUBBER TREE)
3.1 Ecological Profile & Habitat Support
Hevea brasiliensis, while grown extensively in commercial monocultures, supports complex canopy food webs when integrated into agroforestry systems. Epiphytic ferns, mosses, and lichens thrive on its bark, creating mini-ecosystems. The seeds serve as a food source for forest rodents and birds, and its leaf litter enriches soil organic matter, fostering a diverse community of soil decomposers and insects.
3.2 Medicinal & Pharmacological Impact
The latex of Hevea brasiliensis is the primary natural source of rubber, containing cis-1,4-polyisoprene. Beyond industrial uses, the leaf extracts of the rubber tree contain bioactive compounds like phenolics and flavonoids, which exhibit significant antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activities. Traditional communities use rubber tree bark decoctions for treating minor skin infections and rheumatic pains, and modern studies investigate rubber seed oil for its antimicrobial fatty acid profile.
