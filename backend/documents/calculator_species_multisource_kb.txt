=== SPECIES: NEEM | Azadirachta indica | Meliaceae ===
Aliases: Neem, Margosa, Indian Lilac, Nim, Nimba, Arishta
================================================================================
[FAO] Azadirachta indica is recognized by FAO as a flagship multipurpose agroforestry species providing timber, biopesticides, and shade.
[ICRAF] In ICRAF agroforestry, neem provides windbreak boundaries, nitrogenous leaf mulch, and organic pest suppression for crops.
[USDA] USDA Plants profile: Azadirachta indica A.Juss., Meliaceae. Large evergreen tree with high heat and drought resilience in frost-free zones.
[GBIF] GBIF records demonstrate extensive native distribution in South Asia with naturalized clusters across tropical Africa and the Americas.
[India Biodiversity Portal] India Biodiversity Portal: Indigenous across Indian dry plains and plateaus; central to rural village ecology and avenue tree cover.
[FSI] FSI inventories classify Neem among the top 5 Tree Outside Forests (TOF) contributors to India's national carbon stock.
TAXONOMY: Scientific name Azadirachta indica; Family Meliaceae; Genus Azadirachta; Common name Neem.
HABITAT: Dry deciduous forests, thorny scrublands, and open savannah woodlands. Soil: Sandy, stony, clayey, saline, and nutrient-poor dry soils. Climate: Tropical and subtropical dry zones (Köppen: Aw, BSh, Cwa).
MEDICINAL: Broad-spectrum antibacterial, antifungal, antiviral, anti-inflammatory, and insecticidal agent. Used for eczema, acne, gingivitis, wound healing, and blood purification. Key phytochemicals: Limonoids (Azadirachtin, Nimbin, Salannin, Gedunin, Nimbolide), quercetin, and catechins.
CULTURAL: Pooja performed under the trunk with turmeric, vermilion, and red sacred threads for family protection. Sacred in Hinduism; venerated as an incarnation of Goddess Durga, Mariamman, or Sheetala Devi.
PESTS: Diseases: Leaf spot (Colletotrichum gloeosporioides), Powdery mildew (Oidium azadirachtae); Pests: Tea mosquito bug (Helopeltis antonii), Scale insects (Pulvinaria maxima); Treatment: Apply neem oil spray, bio-fungicides like Trichoderma viride, ensure canopy aeration, and avoid waterlogging.
CARBON/CLIMATE: Wood density ~0.72 g/cm³; Sequestration rate: 20kg to 30kg of CO2 per tree per year under mature canopy conditions. Dense canopy intercepts intense solar radiation, lowering ambient microclimate temperatures by 3°C to 5°C.
BIODIVERSITY: Flowers attract native pollinators; canopy provides essential bird and wildlife nesting shelter.

=== SPECIES: ASHOKA | Saraca asoca | Fabaceae ===
Aliases: Ashoka, Sorrowless Tree, Sita Ashok, Jasundi, Asoka
================================================================================
[FAO] Saraca asoca is cataloged in FAO non-wood forest product registries as an irreplaceable medicinal bark and conservation tree.
[ICRAF] In ICRAF moist agroforestry designs, Ashoka serves as a shade-tolerant middle-tier companion tree in sacred groves and buffer zones.
[USDA] USDA Plants profile: Saraca asoca (Roxb.) De Wilde, Fabaceae. Dense evergreen tree with spectacular scarlet flower clusters.
[GBIF] GBIF records highlight localized endemic clusters in the Western Ghats and cultivated temple gardens across tropical Asia.
[India Biodiversity Portal] India Biodiversity Portal: Indigenous sacred tree of high conservation importance with intense cultural presence in southern India.
[FSI] FSI inventories highlight Ashoka as a conservation-priority species requiring strictly monitored sustainable harvesting policies.
TAXONOMY: Scientific name Saraca asoca; Family Fabaceae; Genus Saraca; Common name Ashoka.
HABITAT: Shady understory of tropical rain forests, riverine buffer zones, and coastal green belts. Soil: Deep alluvial, loamy, and humic clay-loam forest soils. Climate: Humid tropical zone (Köppen: Af, Am, monsoon belts).
MEDICINAL: Premier uterine tonic, anti-hemorrhagic, and analgesic agent. Used for menorrhagia, dysmenorrhea, and internal bleeding. Key phytochemicals: Saracin, catechins, epicatechin, leucocyanidin, beta-sitosterol, and condensed tannins.
CULTURAL: Pooja performed by women for family harmony, fertility, and freedom from grief (sorrowless). Sacred in Hinduism, Buddhism, and Jainism. Dedicated to Kama; Buddha born under Ashoka in Lumbini.
PESTS: Diseases: Leaf spot (Pestalotiopsis versicolor), Seedling blight (Phytophthora nicotianae); Pests: Pod borers (Deudorix isocrates), Leaf miners (Acrocercops tetradelta); Treatment: Organic copper fungicides (Bordeaux mixture), neem seed kernel sprays, and pruning of overcrowded twigs.
CARBON/CLIMATE: Wood density ~0.67 g/cm³; Sequestration rate: 12kg to 22kg of CO2 per tree per year in moist tropical settings. Broad evergreen umbrella provides deep acoustic dampening and micro-thermal shadow cooling.
BIODIVERSITY: Flowers attract native pollinators; canopy provides essential bird and wildlife nesting shelter.

=== SPECIES: BAMBOO | Bambusa vulgaris | Poaceae ===
Aliases: Bamboo, Common Bamboo, Golden Bamboo, Bans, Velu
================================================================================
[FAO] FAO identifies Bamboo as a primary strategic bio-economy crop for rapid carbon sequestration and rural livelihood.
[ICRAF] In ICRAF agroforestry, bamboo clumps stabilize riverbanks, shelter farm borders, and provide sustainable annual harvests.
[USDA] USDA Plants profile: Bambusa vulgaris Schrad., Poaceae. Rapidly growing clumping tropical bamboo with high structural utility.
[GBIF] GBIF records demonstrate extensive native and cultivated distribution across tropical Asia, South America, and Africa.
[India Biodiversity Portal] India Biodiversity Portal: Ubiquitous in homesteads, riverbanks, and forests across India; vital to artisanal communities.
[FSI] FSI classifies bamboo as a major non-timber forest resource contributing significantly to national green cover and rural carbon sinks.
TAXONOMY: Scientific name Bambusa vulgaris; Family Poaceae; Genus Bambusa; Common name Bamboo.
HABITAT: Riverbanks, moist forest margins, riparian floodplains, and rural farm boundaries. Soil: Rich alluvial, silty clay-loams, and moist riverine soils. Climate: Tropical and subtropical humid monsoon zones (Köppen: Af, Am, Cwa).
MEDICINAL: Rich in bio-available organic silica (Banslochan / Tabasheer). Treats respiratory distress, joint degeneration, and digestive ulcers. Key phytochemicals: Organic silica (up to 90% in Tabasheer), orientin, vitexin, phenolic acids, and dietary shoot fiber.
CULTURAL: Associated with the divine flute (Bansuri) of Lord Krishna; essential in wedding mandaps and ceremonial canopies. Sacred symbol of flexibility, resilience, and purity in Hindu, Buddhist, and East Asian traditions.
PESTS: Diseases: Bamboo culm rot (Fusarium spp.), Witches' broom (Balansia linearis); Pests: Bamboo culm borer (Dinoderus minutus), Bamboo aphid (Pseudoregma bambusicola); Treatment: Boric-acid borax immersion for felled culms, biocontrol with Beauveria bassiana, and clump thinning.
CARBON/CLIMATE: Wood density ~0.4 g/cm³; Sequestration rate: 35kg to 50kg of CO2 per mature clump per year (extremely high annual vegetative carbon increment). Massive underground rhizome network binds topsoil against torrential monsoon erosion and mudslides.
BIODIVERSITY: Flowers attract native pollinators; canopy provides essential bird and wildlife nesting shelter.
