=== SPECIES: AZADIRACHTA INDICA (NEEM) ===
1. Plant Taxonomy
- Scientific Name: Azadirachta indica A.Juss.
- Common Names: Neem, Margosa, Indian Lilac, Nim, Nimba, Arishta.
- Family: Meliaceae
- Order: Sapindales
- Genus: Azadirachta
- Species: Azadirachta indica
- Botanical Description: A fast-growing evergreen tree reaching 15–20m (up to 35m) with spreading rounded crown, greyish-brown fissured bark, pinnate leaves with serrated margins, fragrant small white flowers in axillary panicles, and smooth yellow drupe fruits.
- Synonyms: Melia azadirachta L., Antelaea azadirachta (L.) Adelb..
- Native Range: Indian subcontinent (India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal) and Myanmar.
- Accepted Classification: Kingdom: Plantae; Division: Tracheophyta; Class: Magnoliopsida; Order: Sapindales; Family: Meliaceae; Genus: Azadirachta; Species: Azadirachta indica. (Verified by Kew POWO, ITIS & Catalogue of Life).

2. Biodiversity & Geographic Distribution
- Species Occurrence & Geographic Distribution: Extensively naturalized across tropical/subtropical Asia, Africa, Caribbean, and northern Australia.
- Biodiversity Hotspots: High occurrence in Western Ghats, central Deccan plateau, and dry plains of Myanmar.
- Observation Records: Over 150,000 georeferenced observations recorded on GBIF and iNaturalist globally.
- Native and Invasive Status: Native to South Asia; naturalized or mildly invasive in parts of Australia and Caribbean scrublands.
- Conservation Status: IUCN Red List Category: Least Concern (LC).

=== SPECIES: SARACA ASOCA (ASHOKA) ===

2. Biodiversity & Geographic Distribution
- Species Occurrence & Geographic Distribution: Endemic to wet evergreen tracts of Western Ghats, Eastern Himalayas, and Sri Lanka.
- Biodiversity Hotspots: Western Ghats biodiversity hotspot represents the core natural genetic sanctuary.
- Observation Records: Over 12,000 verified observation records on GBIF and iNaturalist primarily in southern and western India.
- Native and Invasive Status: Native to South Asia; non-invasive and conservation-dependent in wild tracts.
- Conservation Status: IUCN Red List Category: Vulnerable (VU).

- Species Occurrence & Geographic Distribution: Native to tropical Asia; extensively naturalized across pantropical river valleys.
- Biodiversity Hotspots: Indo-Burma biodiversity hotspot and Western Ghats riparian zones.
- Observation Records: Over 85,000 occurrences on GBIF and iNaturalist across Asia, Africa, and Latin America.
- Native and Invasive Status: Widely cultivated; can spread aggressively along disturbed watercourses but easily managed in clumps.
