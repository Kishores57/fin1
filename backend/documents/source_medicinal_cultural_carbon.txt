=== SPECIES: AZADIRACHTA INDICA (NEEM) ===
1. Medicinal Properties & Phytochemistry
- Medicinal Uses: Broad-spectrum antibacterial, antifungal, antiviral, anti-inflammatory, and insecticidal agent. Used for eczema, acne, gingivitis, wound healing, and blood purification.
- Active Phytochemicals: Limonoids (Azadirachtin, Nimbin, Salannin, Gedunin, Nimbolide), quercetin, and catechins.
- Traditional & Ayurvedic Uses: Known as "Arishta" (reliever of sickness). Used in Nimbadi Kwatha and Nimba Taila to pacify Pitta and Kapha doshas.
- Modern Scientific Evidence: Over 1,200 PubMed studies validating dental plaque reduction, dermatological healing, and organic pest control.
- Toxicity & Side Effects: Safe for external application. High oral doses contraindicated in pregnancy and infants (can induce Reye-like symptoms).

2. Cultural and Spiritual Significance
- Religious Importance: Sacred in Hinduism; venerated as an incarnation of Goddess Durga, Mariamman, or Sheetala Devi.
- Cultural Significance: Pooja performed under the trunk with turmeric, vermilion, and red sacred threads for family protection.
- Festivals & Mythology: Leaves eaten with jaggery on Gudi Padwa symbolizing life triumphs; associated with celestial Amrita.
- Historical Importance: Historically termed the "Village Pharmacy" or "Nature's Drugstore" across rural India.

3. Carbon Sequestration & Biomass
- Biomass & Stored Carbon: Wood density of approximately 0.72 g/cm³. High structural resilience and long-term carbon retention in woody tissues.
- Sequestration Rate: 20kg to 30kg of CO2 per tree per year under mature canopy conditions.
- Climate Mitigation: Dense canopy intercepts intense solar radiation, lowering ambient microclimate temperatures by 3°C to 5°C.

=== SPECIES: SARACA ASOCA (ASHOKA) ===
1. Medicinal Properties & Phytochemistry
- Medicinal Uses: Premier uterine tonic, anti-hemorrhagic, and analgesic agent. Used for menorrhagia, dysmenorrhea, and internal bleeding.
- Active Phytochemicals: Saracin, catechins, epicatechin, leucocyanidin, beta-sitosterol, and condensed tannins.
- Traditional & Ayurvedic Uses: Revered in Charaka and Sushruta Samhitas; primary constituent of Ashokarishta and Ashokadi Vati.
- Modern Scientific Evidence: PubMed pharmacology validates potent uterine-contractile regulation, anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant properties.
- Toxicity & Side Effects: Low toxicity; contra-indicated during early pregnancy due to uterine-stimulating action.

2. Cultural and Spiritual Significance
- Religious Importance: Sacred in Hinduism, Buddhism, and Jainism. Dedicated to Kama; Buddha born under Ashoka in Lumbini.
- Cultural Significance: Pooja performed by women for family harmony, fertility, and freedom from grief (sorrowless).
- Festivals & Mythology: Linked to Yakshi temple sculptures where touch of maiden's foot causes the tree to blossom.
- Historical Importance: Celebrated in Sanskrit literature by Kalidasa; central to royal pleasure gardens of ancient India.

3. Carbon Sequestration & Biomass
- Biomass & Stored Carbon: Wood density of approximately 0.67 g/cm³. High structural resilience and long-term carbon retention in woody tissues.
- Sequestration Rate: 12kg to 22kg of CO2 per tree per year in moist tropical settings.
- Climate Mitigation: Broad evergreen umbrella provides deep acoustic dampening and micro-thermal shadow cooling.

=== SPECIES: BAMBUSA VULGARIS (BAMBOO) ===
1. Medicinal Properties & Phytochemistry
- Medicinal Uses: Rich in bio-available organic silica (Banslochan / Tabasheer). Treats respiratory distress, joint degeneration, and digestive ulcers.
- Active Phytochemicals: Organic silica (up to 90% in Tabasheer), orientin, vitexin, phenolic acids, and dietary shoot fiber.
- Traditional & Ayurvedic Uses: Known as "Vamsha" in Ayurveda. Formulated in Sitopaladi Churna for cough, bronchial asthma, and vitality restoration.
- Modern Scientific Evidence: PubMed studies demonstrate exceptional bone remineralization, antioxidant capacity, and anti-hyperlipidemic activity of shoot extracts.
- Toxicity & Side Effects: Raw young shoots contain cyanogenic glycosides; must be boiled or fermented before consumption.

2. Cultural and Spiritual Significance
- Religious Importance: Sacred symbol of flexibility, resilience, and purity in Hindu, Buddhist, and East Asian traditions.
- Cultural Significance: Associated with the divine flute (Bansuri) of Lord Krishna; essential in wedding mandaps and ceremonial canopies.
- Festivals & Mythology: Used in Chhath Pooja and regional harvest festivals across India; symbolizes unbroken family lineage.
- Historical Importance: The backbone of rural Asian material culture: bridges, housing, scaffolding, and artisan crafts.

3. Carbon Sequestration & Biomass
- Biomass & Stored Carbon: Wood density of approximately 0.4 g/cm³. High structural resilience and long-term carbon retention in woody tissues.
- Sequestration Rate: 35kg to 50kg of CO2 per mature clump per year (extremely high annual vegetative carbon increment).
- Climate Mitigation: Massive underground rhizome network binds topsoil against torrential monsoon erosion and mudslides.
